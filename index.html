      Method and sources

      Sea freight is modelled per container as (base + rate × nautical miles) × a trade-lane factor, calibrated so Shanghai–Rotterdam, Shanghai–Genoa, Shanghai–Los Angeles and Shanghai–New York match the Drewry World Container Index of 1 Oct 2026 ($3,399, $3,702, $7,835 and $10,428 per 40ft). Other lanes are model estimates. Sea distance follows the drawn lane network; days use 15 knots plus port time, feeder transshipment and customs.

      Road distances between cities and ports come from Google Maps (Shanghai city–Yangshan and Busan are estimates). Truck costs use regional per-km estimates plus border fees. Rail uses the ERAI China–Europe average of about $10,100 per 40ft (July 2026); Middle Corridor rates are estimates. Air uses a distance-based rate inside the 2026 Asia–Europe range of $3.20–6.50 per kg (source).

      Risk: chokepoint status from carrier updates on the Red Sea and Suez and Hormuz war-risk premiums. War-risk surcharges, delay probabilities and insurance add-ons are model assumptions. Routes through Russia or Belarus are flagged for sanctions and excluded from the recommendation. This tool gives planning estimates, not quotes; customs duties and taxes are not included.

      Containers: typical ISO internal dimensions and maximum payloads (20ft 5.90 × 2.35 × 2.39 m, 28,200 kg; 40ft 12.03 × 2.35 × 2.39 m, 26,700 kg; 40ft high cube 2.70 m high, 26,500 kg). Carriers' boxes vary by a few centimetres and kilograms. Loading is calculated lane by lane, testing lengthwise and crosswise placement; it does not model weight distribution or securing. LCL pricing (per revenue ton, about 2.5 times the full-container rate per m³ plus freight-station fees) is a model assumption.
